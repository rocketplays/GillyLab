Matchup Analytics — state of play
================================================================================
2026-07-16, second pass. The feature is deployed and was rebuilt today: it is now
a MODAL HUB opened from inside Fight Info, not a dropdown under the bout. It has
been seen in a browser, on desktop and on a phone, which it never had been before.

WHAT THE FEATURE IS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A "Matchup Analytics Deep Dive ›" button INSIDE each bout's Fight Info dropdown,
sitting between the tale of the tape and Style — the seam where a reader stops
taking in who these men are and starts asking what they do. It opens a 1040px
modal with TWO tabs: Striking (lands here) and Grappling.

THERE IS NO OVERVIEW TAB AND NO FOOTER. Both were built, both were cut same-day.
  * Overview's grappling half was a byte-for-byte duplicate of the Grappling tab.
    Its striking half was the AGGREGATE of the very cross-tab the Striking tab
    breaks out cell by cell — a landing tab whose job was to average away the thing
    the next tab shows you properly. Exactly ONE number on it existed nowhere else:
    knockdowns, which aren't in the grid. Knockdowns now sit at the foot of "Where
    his offense goes". Strikes landed and strike accuracy are GONE OUTRIGHT — both
    are just the grid's nine cells added back up, i.e. the averaging this feature
    exists to undo, printed directly above the grid that undoes it. (A short-lived
    "The totals" section carried them for one commit before that landed.)
    Striking now lands, and when the striking is level it points at Grappling —
    10 of the 50 renderable bouts on the card are that case.
  * The footer carried provenance ("last N UFC fights each, from official box
    scores") and the tick's meaning. Both moved onto the section headers that own
    them, which is also where the N is HONEST: the totals count FIGHT_STATS fights
    and the grid counts FIGHT_GRID fights, and one footer had to pick one and be
    wrong about the other.

It crosses one fighter's offense against the other's defense — the thing the Fight
Info panel structurally cannot do, because it only ever reads a fighter's own
numbers. It renders counts, and a two-sentence read generated from the data:

    "Usman has 50 minutes of control time across those fights; Du Plessis has 16.
     Usman is far harder to take down: 27 of 32 stopped, against 8 of 23."

40 of 50 bouts get a read; 10 say nothing, which is correct — most fights are not
lopsided, and a panel that always has a verdict will invent one. ddRead() now
takes a `domain` ('strike'|'grap'|null) so each tab leads with its own strongest
line; it PARTITIONS THE EXISTING CANDIDATES and adds no new claim. Striking leads
on 30 of 50, grappling on 32.


WHY IT STOPPED BEING A DROPDOWN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Two reasons, and the second is the real one.
  * A second full-width bar under every row read as chrome. The button was both in
    the way and easy to miss.
  * A 3x3 cross-tab PER FIGHTER does not fit in a card column — so v1 didn't show
    it. It printed four of the nine cells as a flat list, which is exactly the
    averaged-into-mush the grid exists to undo. THE CONTAINER WAS QUIETLY DECIDING
    WHAT THE FEATURE WAS ALLOWED TO SAY.

Moving it inside Fight Info also killed the two-fightRow trap for this button:
renderScouting() is ONE function, so the entry point is emitted once. The trap is
still live for anything else on a fight row.

    fight-grid.json               239KB, still card-scoped, unchanged
    fight-stats.json              7909KB — same as before the sweep. Not one byte.
    grid-names.json               2.2KB -> 9.1KB (eager)

    accuracy cells with a median  16/99 -> 63/99   allow 59/99   aim 99/99

Six stragglers remain "to process" for ever: they are on ACTIVE_ROSTER, have stats,

DON'T REPEAT MY MISTAKE ABOUT THE NETWORK. I claimed twice, in a commit message and
in this file, that the sandbox had no route to ESPN and the sweep had to run on the
Mac. FALSE. I curled site-api.espn.com, got 000, and generalised one host to the
whole domain. The scraper uses sports.core.api.espn.com (CORE/ATHL in
fight-stats-backfill.py) and site.api.espn.com — both answer 200. Test the host the
code actually calls before concluding the network is the problem.

BACKGROUNDING DOESN'T SURVIVE, chunking does. setsid/nohup processes are killed at
the boundary of each tool call; `pgrep -f fight-stats-parallel` reports them alive
because it matches the checking command's OWN command line. Use ps aux | grep '[f]'
or check the output file's mtime.

WHY IT WAS NEEDED. Shading a cell "vs your division" needs ~8 peers in that
division AND that cell. Measured on the card-only grid (109 fighters, 11 divisions):
    division x cell combos with >= 8 peers:   16 of 99
    the entire clinch row:                     0 divisions
Worse, a "division median" drawn from card fighters is really "the median of
whoever from that weight class is booked this weekend" — it moves week to week for
reasons that have nothing to do with the fighter being shaded. Post-sweep, clinch
.head/body cleared in 9 and 7 divisions respectively; clinch.leg / ground.body /
ground.leg are still empty and always will be, because nobody throws 25 leg kicks
from the clinch. Those show "—", which is correct.

AND BE CLEAR WHAT IT BUYS, because it is not as much as it sounds. Division
explains only 12–19% of the variance in these rates:
    head at distance   between-division means spread 2.3 pts, within-division 6.3
    what lands on him  between 3.1, within 6.5
    leg at distance    between 3.5, within 8.4
It is the right comparison and it is a small one. The honest gain is that a
heavyweight stops being marked down for heavyweight output.


THE DATA, AND WHY IT'S IN FOUR FILES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
    data/grid-names.json      9.1KB  eager     manifest + baselines + div medians
    data/fight-grid.json      239KB  on click  the 3x3 cross-tab, CARD FIGHTERS ONLY
    data/fight-grid-all.json  1436KB never     MASTER — build-time only
    data/fight-stats.json     7909KB eager     UNCHANGED — not one byte added

THE MASTER IS THE SWEEP'S OUTPUT AND IT MUST NOT SHIP. 618 fighters is ~1.4MB and
the panel would download all of it on every click to show two men — ~84% waste —
while CI rewrote 1.4MB twice a day into a .git that is already 825MB. What the
sweep is FOR is the medians, and the medians are 9KB. So: population in the master,
conclusions in the manifest, and the wire stays thin.

  * fight-grid-all.json is TRACKED (CI must persist it between runs; without it the
    master rebuilds from whatever delta the backfill last fetched) but NOT SERVED
    (excluded by name in build-site.sh).
  * IT CANNOT USE THE data/_ PREFIX. That prefix does exclude it from public/ —
    and .gitignore line 10 is `data/_*.json`, so it would also stop being tracked,
    which is the exact disaster above. "Not shipped" and "not tracked" are the same
    prefix in this repo and this file needs one without the other.
  * grid-names.json now carries `divBase` ({div: {acc,allow,aim}} x 9 cells, each
    [median, spread, n] or null) and `divs` (name -> division, so the browser
    doesn't scan 3,101 FIGHTERS rows on every open).

MEDIAN + IQR/1.349, NOT MEAN + SD. The median because these rates are right-skewed
and a mean is dragged up by the few extremes — statDivMedians() already made this
call for the profile bars and says so. The IQR because the spread has to match the
centre: an sd is computed around the MEAN and inflated by the very outliers the
median was chosen to ignore. Rescaled by 1.349 so it reads on the sd scale.

index.html eager-fetches fight-stats.json on every page load. The grid would add
~2MB to that for a panel you have to click, so it lives apart and loads on press.
The manifest exists because the BUTTON'S VISIBILITY is decided while rendering
every bout — answering that from fight-grid.json would mean a 245KB fetch on page
load to decide the visibility of a button most people never press.

THE GRID IS THE POINT. ESPN serves head/body/leg BY distance/clinch/ground and the
backfill was summing it into margins and throwing the cross-tab away. Measured
across 63 card fighters:
    head accuracy AT DISTANCE    mean 35.8%  sd 4.9
    head accuracy ON THE GROUND  mean 70.2%  sd 8.2
    corr(the two)                -0.04
Zero correlation — unrelated skills. "Head 79%" reads identically for a boxer and
a ground-and-pounder. Mackenzie Dern is +55 points better on the ground than at
range; a kickboxer is +11. The margin reported one number for both.

TWO THINGS THE PANEL SHADED OR COLOURED THAT IT HAD NOT EARNED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Both were found by screenshotting the thing and looking at it. Neither would have
been caught by any test that checks the numbers, because the NUMBERS WERE RIGHT.

 1. THE GRID WAS SHADED ON THE ABSOLUTE RATE, and every fighter came out the same
    wall of green — because head-at-range is ~35% for EVERYONE and leg kicks are
    ~82% for EVERYONE. The palette was encoding WHICH TARGET THE CELL WAS, not one
    thing about the man in it. Measured over the 109 fighters in the grid:
        dist.head    n=105  mean 35.3%  sd 6.6   (spread 16–52%)
        dist.body    n= 77  mean 68.2%  sd 9.6
        dist.leg     n= 57  mean 81.7%  sd 9.2   (spread 57–95%)
        clinch.head  n= 32  mean 64.5%  sd 10.5
        ground.head  n= 55  mean 69.5%  sd 10.2  (spread 39–88%)
        clinch.leg / ground.body / ground.leg — too few fighters clear the floor
    Between cells: 46 points. Within a cell, where the fighter lives: 7–10. A flat
    scale spends its whole range on the former. Cells are now shaded against the
    per-cell card norm (mhCellBase(), computed client-side from FIGHT_GRID), which
    is the only comparison that says anything about this man. Diverging: orange
    below the norm, green above, capped at 2sd.
    (corr(head-at-distance, head-on-ground) re-checked on today's file: -0.010
    over the 55 fighters with both. The premise holds.)

 2. GREEN IS A CLAIM AND IT WAS FIRING ON NOISE. The first render painted Usman's
    50% strike accuracy green against Du Plessis's 49% — a one-point gap on ~1200
    attempts, inside the noise by a factor of two. That is the "won't be wrestled"
    overclaim wearing a colour instead of a verb. The accent is now gated:
        rates            -> two-proportion standard error, gap >= 2se
        counts of events -> Poisson, |a-b| >= 2*sqrt(a+b)
        magnitudes       -> ratio >= 1.25 (control time; it has no n)
        volume, share, "what lands on him", "time under control" -> NEVER by lead
    Neither test is tuned. Only the COLOUR is gated — the numbers print either way.
    On Du Plessis/Usman exactly two bars now light: takedowns stopped and control
    time, which are the two facts the prose read leads with. The colour and the
    sentence agree because both answer to the same discipline.

 3. AND THEN I WROTE THE SAME BUG AGAIN, IN THE SAME FEATURE, ONE LAYER OVER.
    The division grade shipped as `rate < median ? green : red` — which painted
    Usman RED for allowing 31% at range against a welterweight median of 31%. Dead
    average, marked down. EVERY fighter is on one side of a median, so a bare
    comparison colours the whole card and says nothing. _mhGrade() now needs the gap
    to clear two bars that answer different questions:
        >= one division spread  — is he unusual FOR HIS DIVISION?
        >= 2 standard errors    — is his own rate real, or 25 attempts of noise?
    Result across the card: 34 green, 26 red, 215 neutral (78%). The colour means
    something when it appears, and "ordinary" is the truthful answer for most men.
    THE LESSON THAT DIDN'T TAKE THE FIRST TIME: a threshold fixed on one code path
    is not a principle until it's on all of them. lead and grade are the same claim
    about different populations, and only one of them had been taught to shut up.


TRAPS — ALL OF THESE WERE REAL BUGS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  * THERE ARE TWO fightRow FUNCTIONS. renderHomeFightCard() draws the featured
    card; renderCarousel() draws the upcoming cards. Patching one put the button on
    one card and nowhere else, which reads as "the feature doesn't exist" — and did.
    Every change to a fight row is two changes. This is worth actually fixing.
    (No longer bites Matchup Analytics — it lives in renderScouting now.)
  * scoutingFightPanelHTML() READ f.wc, WHICH NOTHING HAS EVER SET. Both renderers
    carry the weight class on f.label; neither writes f.wc. So data-wc has been ""
    on every bout on the site and renderScouting's `weightClass` argument has always
    been the empty string. Nothing printed it, so nothing looked broken — it
    surfaced only when the hub header asked for it. Now derived from f.label.
  * NAME JOINS. Written wrong THREE times in one day: "Dricus Du Plessis" vs
    "Dricus du Plessis", "Aleksandar Rakic" vs "Rakić". Every lookup must normalise
    (NFD, strip diacritics, lowercase). A miss looks exactly like absent data.
  * `" " in name` DROPS MONONYMS. Sumudaerji, Aoriqileng — 10+ fights each, silently
    excluded. Read bouts[].fighters[].fighterName; don't hunt for keys called "name"
    and then filter the junk.
  * --only-missing CANNOT SEE THIS. Its predicate is "have we ever saved this
    fighter" and every card fighter is already saved — without the grid. Use
    --needs-grid, which asks the actual question and is self-clearing (no mark file).
  * split-fight-grid.cjs MUST MERGE. fight-grid.json is the ONLY copy of the grid;
    rebuilding it from scratch each run deletes everyone not in that batch.
  * THE MANIFEST MUST BE WRITTEN ON NO-OP RUNS. Steady state IS a no-op run, so a
    manifest that only regenerates when there's fresh data goes stale on exactly the
    schedule it serves, and every button vanishes on the first quiet day.
  * THE $FILES ALLOWLIST in update-odds.yml is explicit. A new data file that isn't
    in it gets generated and never committed. Green job, starved feature.
  * build-site.sh SHIPS data/*.json WHOLESALE (git ls-files minus a few patterns).
    A build-time-only file is served to every visitor unless you exclude it BY NAME
    — see fight-grid-all.json. This is the same bug prototypes/ has open below.
  * roster_names() IS NOT THE ACTIVE ROSTER. It is every name in FIGHTERS: 3,101,
    of which ~2,450 are retired. Use --active for anything that becomes a baseline.
  * grid_index() IS THE SELF-CLEARING PREDICATE AND IT READS THE GRID FILES. It has
    now broken TWICE, the same way, for two different reasons — read its docstring
    before touching anything that decides where the grid lives. First time: someone
    pointed it at fight-stats.json, which the split empties by design, so every
    fighter looked missing for ever. Second time (me): fight-grid.json used to BE
    the master, the division work made it the CARD SUBSET, and the predicate could
    suddenly only see 109 of ~500 swept fighters. Observed live — the queue went UP,
    340 -> 447, straight after a split. Left alone the sweep never terminates and CI
    refetches the same men twice a day, green throughout. It now reads the master
    first and unions the shipped subset. IF YOU MOVE THE GRID AGAIN, MOVE THIS TOO.
  * ACTIVE_ROSTER USES DISPLAY NAMES AND IS ON ONE LINE. Folding accents is not
    enough — "King Green" -> "Bobby Green", "Patricio Freire" -> "Patrício Pitbull",
    "Thomas Gantt" -> "Tommy Gantt" only resolve through ACTIVE_ROSTER_ALIASES; 7
    fighters including a former champion silently vanish without it. And the array
    closes with `"];` on ONE line, so a /\[(.*?)\n\s*\];/ pattern runs past the end
    of it into ROSTER_CHANGES and starts collecting week labels as fighter names.
    Both of these bit on the first write of active_roster_names(), in one function.


DEAD END — DO NOT REBUILD THIS. `adv` AND `slams` ARE EMPTY.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The grid file carries `adv` (advances to half guard/side/mount/back) and `slams` on
every row, and they look like treasure: the only fields in this data that could say
what a man DOES with a takedown once he has it, which control time cannot
distinguish from lying in someone's guard. I wrote a whole "Positions gained"
section on that reasoning. Then measured it, across all 621 card fighter-fights:

    rows with the adv array present : 621  (100%)
    rows with ANY advance > 0       :   4  (0.6%)
    totals [halfguard, side, mount, back] : [2, 4, 0, 0]
    rows with a slam                :   0  (total slams: 0)

ESPN serves the keys and fills them with zeros. Six advances and no slams across
621 fights is not under-read data, it is an empty column — and the section would
have rendered "Mount 0 · Back 0" for everyone and presented that emptiness as a
finding about the fighters. Both fields are no longer collected in _ddGrid().

The tell was the usual one: THE MECHANISM ARRIVED BEFORE THE MEASUREMENT and
explained the data a little too well. If you want this section it needs a source
that actually has the numbers; it is not sitting in this file waiting.

kd / sub / rev survived the same check and are shown: over those 621 rows kd is
non-zero on 22.2%, sub on 22.4%, rev on 9.3%, control time on 87.6%. Sparse, but
real. Rows where BOTH men are at zero are dropped rather than printed as "0 vs 0",
which would read as a measured tie instead of the absence it is.

THE LESSON WORTH CARRYING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Three times today I invented a mechanism, found it plausible, wrote it down in past
tense, and shipped it — the chin "bug" that did not exist (corr with career length
= 0.059, i.e. nothing), the "legs are a narrow band" story behind the z-score change
(head sd 8.1, leg sd 9.2 — the same), and "postinstalls are the deploy failure" (the
binaries were there all along). Each sounded right. Each took one cheap measurement
to kill. THE TELL IS ALWAYS THE SAME: the mechanism arrives BEFORE the measurement
and explains the data a little too well.

And the floor: N=500 for a cell, more for a difference. N=120 read one change as
"46% -> 33%" when the truth was "46% -> 40%". It caught me four times in the session
where I raised it.

2026-07-16, second pass — it caught me twice more, and BOTH TIMES IN THE PALETTE
rather than in the numbers. "Advances have been sitting in this file unread" and
"shade the cells by accuracy" were each a mechanism that arrived before its
measurement; one was an empty column, the other was colouring the axis instead of
the fighter. Neither would have failed a test of the numbers, because the numbers
were right in both. THE NEW TELL: a rendering decision is a claim too. Shading,
accenting and ordering all assert something, and they get audited by screenshotting
the thing and looking at it — which, until today, nobody had ever done.
