Six Degrees of the Octagon — DEAD. Do not rebuild.
==================================================
2026-07-15. Prototype built, playtested, killed the same day. This file exists so
nobody re-pitches it in six months, because it is a genuinely good idea and it
WILL get re-pitched.

The idea: your 58,518 bout rows are a social graph. 22,432 fighters, fully
connected, most pairs ~4 hops apart. Give people two fighters, make them build
the chain. Bogdan Guskov -> Ryan Spann -> Rogerio Nogueira -> Kazushi Sakuraba ->
Royce Gracie: a man fighting this month is four handshakes from UFC 1. Nobody
else has shipped this. It's unique, it's ours, and it's dead.


WHY IT'S DEAD
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Not because of bugs. Because the bugs can't be BOUNDED.

Six Degrees is the only thing on the site that asserts a RELATIONSHIP between two
fighters. Everything else — profiles, scouting, lite pages, the tracker — shows
one fighter's own record. A bad row on a profile is survivable and fixable when
someone reports it. In Six Degrees, a bad row IS the puzzle, and the puzzle is
the product. Same data, categorically different blast radius.

And the data cannot support that assertion. Two separate bug classes were found
in one afternoon, each AFTER the data had been declared clean:

  1. ONE-SIDED COLLISION. Brendan Allen "fought" a flyweight Bruno Silva. Caught
     by a reciprocity gate — a real bout appears in both corners' records.
     Found by a playtester, not a test.

  2. RECIPROCAL COLLISION. Ryan Parker fought Remco Pardoel at UFC 7 in 1995 and
     also lost to Donte Johnson on a regional card in 2025. Two men, one key —
     and EVERY EDGE RECIPROCATES, because both halves are real fights. The gate
     is structurally blind to it. All 48 tests passed while this sat on the board.
     Found only by asking an unrelated question (who has an implausible career
     span?).

Two classes, two accidents. There is no reason to believe there isn't a third.
A game whose entire pitch is "these two men really are connected" dies the first
time someone sees Conor McGregor bridged to 1995 through a phantom — and it takes
the site's credibility with it, including the parts that earn money.


THE ESCAPE HATCH DOESN'T EXIST
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The obvious fix is to stop keying identity on names and use stable IDs. I said
that during the session. I was wrong, and this is the most important paragraph
in the file:

    ESPN athletes on file:              496
    distinct people in FIGHT_HISTORY: 22,443   -> ESPN covers 2%
    ...with UFC experience:            3,269   -> ESPN covers 15%

The graph is overwhelmingly regional fighters ESPN has never heard of. And the
lookup that ASSIGNS an ID is itself by name — so "Bruno Silva" resolves to one
ID and the collision survives the migration completely intact. An ID merge would
cost months and not fix the thing it's supposed to fix.

This is not a GillyLab problem. MMA has no central identity registry. Nobody's
database has clean identities for 22,000 regional fighters — not Tapology's, not
the competitors'. That is probably why nobody has built Six Degrees despite the
idea being obvious to anyone who looks at the data.

So: not "parked pending a big job." Dead.


WHAT WOULD UNPARK IT
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A source of per-bout fighter IDs covering the regional circuit, which does not
currently exist at any price. If that ever changes, the work is ~a day: the
generator, the era scoping, the prototype and 48 passing tests are all still in
the repo.

    prototypes/six-degrees.html          the game (click-to-navigate, no typing)
    prototypes/sixdegrees-data.json      the board
    scripts/gen-sixdegrees-data.cjs      the generator + reciprocity gate
    scripts/test-sixdegrees.cjs          48 checks, driven through the real page

Do NOT resurrect it by "just auditing the fighters." 2,917 board nodes, and the
audit only finds the classes you already thought of. That's the trap.


WHAT THE PROTOTYPE ACTUALLY BOUGHT — all of it kept
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The prototype's job turned out to be finding the bug, and it did:

  - Bruno "Blindado" Silva has a real record (36 bouts, 23-13). He had NONE.
  - The flyweight is untangled; the two men shared 24 opponents, now zero.
  - Jose/José Aldo + Viviane Araujo/Araújo merged. Both canonical records GAINED
    10 closing lines that were stranded in dead shards.
  - scripts/audit-name-links.cjs: splits one-sided links into aliases vs
    collisions by DATE, not by name. 481 real fights recovered, 180 collisions
    identified and triaged (69 touch an active fighter; "Jean Silva" x7 is next).
  - data/name-aliases.json + a CI guard that names the next Bruno on day one
    instead of eight months later.

Five permanent wins on the real product, from a game that never shipped. That's
a good trade. The lesson isn't "don't prototype" — it's that a prototype's
highest-value output is sometimes the thing it breaks.


THE DESIGN NOTES, FOR WHATEVER COMES NEXT
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Four rounds of "still too hard", each fix measured and each still wrong:

  - Typing is the enemy. Free recall from 3,269 names is brutal. Click, never
    type — recognition, not recall. It's Wikirace, not Wordle.
  - Recognition fixes READING, not ROUTING. Knowing which of 41 opponents bends
    toward the target needs the TARGET's opponent list. Blind: 18% solve rate.
    With the dossier: 52%.
  - Don't make people do set-intersection by eye. That's a chore, not a game.
    Highlighting the overlap took it to 73%. Worth noting: my "floor" simulation
    had ALREADY assumed the player finds every overlap for free — I was
    measuring a highlighted game and shipping an unhighlighted one.
  - Stock the on-ramp, not the hardest tier. Random star pairs land on par 4-5
    two-thirds of the time; par 4-5 is ~39% solvable. The first build was 38 of
    60 puzzles at par 4+ and a playtester went 0-for. That was the pool's fault,
    not the player's.
  - Era scoping is the biggest difficulty lever there is — bigger than par,
    bigger than the budget. An all-time board asks you to know 25 years of MMA
    no matter how it's sorted.
  - A game you cannot lose is not a game. The first build had no fail state at
    all, so no pick could be "wrong".

Every one of those was found by someone PLAYING, never by me measuring. That is
the actual lesson of the day.
